10 Questions to Ask When You're Thinking About Downsizing

Don't Wait, Plan Now: A Guide to What's Next

Answers from Valerie Van Pelt, REALTOR® and Seniors Real Estate Specialist®, founder of Collective Moves.

Questions and Answers

1. How do I know when it's time to downsize?

Often it's when the house takes more work to keep up than you want to give it, or when you'd rather live closer to family and friends.

Start with what you want daily life to look like. Are stairs, yardwork, or repairs taking more energy than they used to? Would you like to be closer to friends, family, or the things you enjoy? You can start exploring while you still feel comfortable at home and have time to make decisions at your own pace.

2. Where do I start if the whole idea feels overwhelming?

Pick the question that's weighing on you most. If it's money, start with an estimate of what your home could sell for. If it's where you'd live, tour a place that interests you. If it's your belongings, start with one drawer or closet. One answer can make the next decision easier.

3. What kind of place would feel right for me?

The right place fits your everyday routine and gives you as much help as you want now, or might need later.

Picture an ordinary Tuesday. Where would you walk the dog? How would you get to the grocery store or see friends? Would you rather have a smaller home to yourself or a community with meals, activities, and neighbors nearby? If you're touring senior living communities, ask what's included and what support is available if your needs change. For independent guidance on aging services in Dallas, The Senior Source is a trusted local nonprofit.

4. What can I afford, and how much would I actually keep from selling my home?

What you'd keep is roughly your sale price minus selling costs and any mortgage payoff, and I can estimate that number before you list.

A seller net sheet puts that estimate in writing. Then compare your current expenses with the full cost of your next home, including any deposits, monthly fees, and services you'd pay for separately. Those numbers give you and your financial advisor a starting point.

5. Do I have to sell my house before I move?

Not necessarily: if you can comfortably cover both homes for a while, moving first can work well.

Moving first can give you time to settle in while your home is being prepared for sale. The decision comes down to what you can comfortably carry if the two homes overlap. I help families compare both timelines and, when a senior living community is involved, coordinate with its team around the move-in date.

6. Do I need to update my house before putting it on the market?

Usually less than you'd think, since many homes need clearing out and a few repairs rather than a remodel.

I'd walk through your home with you before you spend money. We'll look at what buyers in your market are likely to notice and what's worth the effort. In some homes, clearing rooms and making a few repairs are the best places to start.

7. What do I do with a lifetime of belongings?

Take what you'll use and love in your next home, and find good homes for the rest through family, sales, or donation.

Start with what you want in your next home. A floor plan can help you see which furniture fits. Ask family members what they'd genuinely like to keep, then decide what to sell, donate, or pass along. If sorting everything yourself feels like too much, a senior move manager or organizer can help.

8. How do I talk with my adult children about the move?

Bring them in early, and give each of them a specific way to help.

Tell them what you're considering, what you're unsure about, and where you'd welcome help. You might say, “I'd like you to tour a couple of places with me. I'm still deciding what feels right.” Giving everyone a role can make the conversation easier while keeping your wishes at the center.

9. How do I prepare to leave a home I still love?

Give yourself time to say goodbye. Take photos of the rooms you love, invite family over for one more meal, or choose a few things you'll enjoy seeing every day in your next home. You can look forward to a move and still miss the home you're leaving.
